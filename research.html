Research

Sweeter ways to use what we waste

My Ph.D. work asked a simple question: can the sugary leftovers from food production become something more valuable?

At Cornell I engineered enzymes with a "sticky" cellulose-binding tag, so they could be fixed onto cheap cellulose and reused again and again. I used them to turn dairy by-products into rare sugars like D-tagatose and allulose, which taste like sugar with far fewer calories. Then I asked consumers what they thought. Happily, they liked the yogurt.
